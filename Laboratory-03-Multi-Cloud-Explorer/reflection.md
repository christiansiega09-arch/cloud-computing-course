Google Cloud's emphasis on AI, machine learning, data analytics, and Kubernetes is what most impressed me about it. Because they all offer on-demand computing, storage, networking, databases, identity and security services, and tools for creating scalable applications, AWS, Azure, and GCP are comparable. Additionally, they offer web consoles and command-line tools for resource management, and they use regions and zones to assist clients in creating dependable cloud systems. The choice of cloud platform is heavily influenced by business requirements since an organization must take into account factors like budget, current technologies, performance, security, compliance, geographic reach, and the expertise of its IT personnel. For instance, GCP can be particularly appealing for AI and Kubernetes workloads, Azure is a logical choice for a company already invested in Microsoft technologies, and AWS is a powerful general-purpose option with a wide range of services. I learned from working as a Cloud Solutions Consultant that picking a cloud provider should take into account the client's actual technical and business needs rather than just going with the most popular platform. Additionally, I discovered that evaluating multi-cloud options is made simpler by the ability to compare comparable services across providers. Because the work is arranged into a specific laboratory folder with research files, comparison tables, client recommendations, a decision matrix, screenshots, and a reflection, my GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio has improved. I was able to make the connection between cloud theory and technical documentation and real-world decision-making thanks to this exercise.
